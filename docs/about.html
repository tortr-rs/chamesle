About

People usually don't leave Linux because of anything big. It's something small: a driver, an app they can't find, a menu that isn't where they expected. So the idea is simple. Make a system that looks like what people know, has the drivers and apps already sorted, and stays out of the way.

Chamesle is pronounced /tʃa.mes.le/. It's a small project, so things move at that speed.

Roadmap

  1. 0.1
    Xfce live ISO, Windows-like layout, Flathub, firmware and drivers.
  2. 0.2
    Installer (dual boot, encryption), the system on apk and the Chamesle repo, .deb and .rpm support.
  3. 0.3
    A GNOME edition. (The update notifier and driver manager came early, in 0.2.)
  4. 0.4
    Updates can't break your computer. Before every update Chamesle takes a snapshot of the system, and if something goes wrong you pick "before the update" in the boot menu and you're back. Only the system is snapshotted, never your files, and only the last 3 are kept, so they don't eat your disk. Plus the first Flavor: Chamesle Astro, for astronomy.
  5. 0.5
    A KDE Plasma edition, and a friendlier start: our own login screen on every edition, a boot menu that's easy to read, and a blue screen that says what went wrong if the system ever crashes, instead of a wall of text.
  6. 0.6
    Jiggity, our own installer. One 620 MB download for every desktop, like Debian's netinst: Jiggity asks everything up front, your language, network, account, disk and which desktop you want, then downloads and installs it, and you restart into it. A new server edition is one of the choices. Chamesle also gets lighter: faster to start and less memory used. Plus three Flavors: a gaming one with Steam, Proton and controllers ready to go, Ranni, KDE Plasma set up to feel like a Mac: a menu bar at the top, a dock at the bottom and Spotlight-style search, and TTY, the console with floating windows on top, on ttywm, our own window manager. And a faster kernel: CachyOS's LTS kernel, tuned for desktops, with a QR code on the crash screen that turns into a readable crash report.
  7. 0.7
    Be Kool: a look of our own. Chamesle Glass on every edition except GNOME: glass frames and taskbar, glossy buttons, Oxygen icons and a new logo. A glass sign-in screen, and a better store: screenshots, ratings and reviews, updates in the background and firmware updates for your BIOS and SSD.
  8. 0.8
    Chamesle's own apps in every language, and a pass on accessibility: screen reader, high contrast and large text from the first boot.
  9. 0.9
    Release candidate: no new features, just fixes. Try it on your computer and tell us on the board what breaks, so 1.0 works on as many machines as possible.
  10. 1.0
    Godrick, with Serp: a desktop of our own, made for Chamesle from the ground up, and on X like the rest. Serp gets a public beta before 1.0, so you can try it and tell us what to fix. Jiggity gets an expert page, for building your own install the way Debian and Fedora let you: untick the apps you don't want, start from the bare base, lay out the disk yourself. App groups (office, games, development) become packages you can add or drop later with one command. And a free software only install: the Linux-libre kernel, no closed drivers or firmware, no Steam, and Flathub limited to free apps. It follows the FSF's guidelines for free distributions. Some Wi-Fi and graphics cards won't work with it, and Jiggity tells you which of yours before you pick it.

No dates for these. Once 1.0 is out, there's a new ISO every 6 months, and the version names follow Elden Ring's bosses: Margit until 1.0, then Godrick, Rennala and Radahn.

Updates every six months

The package repo moves forward twice a year. In between it stays put, so what works on Monday still works on Friday. Security fixes don't wait for that, they go out as soon as they're ready, and so do fixes to Chamesle's own apps. Every update has to install and boot in a test machine before it goes out. The kernel stays on the long-term support line.

We run on X

Chamesle believes in X. Every desktop we ship runs on Xorg, GNOME and KDE Plasma included, even though both of them would rather you used Wayland now. We keep Wayland out wherever we can.

Why: X works. Screen sharing, screen readers, remote desktop, global hotkeys, color pickers, xdotool, old games, the program you wrote in 2004: all of it just works, on every graphics card, without anyone having to write a new protocol for it first. Thirty years of software expects X, and we'd rather spend our time on the desktop than on finding out what broke this week.

For a lot of us, X on Linux meant XFree86. Through the 90s and into the 2000s it was the X server on nearly every Linux and BSD PC, and this website is dressed like its old homepage on purpose. In 2004 the X.Org Foundation took XFree86's code and carried it on, and that X.Org server is what every Chamesle desktop runs today. So when you sign in to Chamesle, it's XFree86's code, still going.

Coming: X8664

X's weak spot is the thing Wayland people always bring up: tearing, stutter, frames that arrive late. We think that's fixable without throwing X away. X8664 is our own X server, a fork of XFree86 4.8, and the goal is X that's as smooth as Wayland while every X program keeps working. It's only just started. Here's the plan.

If you want Wayland anyway, it's your computer: the Wayland sessions are in the repo and the sign-in screen lists whatever you install.

How it's built

Each ISO is made by one script, build.sh, from a list of packages per edition. The init system is dinit, the boot is GRUB with a small initramfs of our own, and the desktop look is Chamesle Glass, our own theme, with the Oxygen icons. All of it is in the GitHub repo if you want to build your own.

The package repo is built by our own tools: every package is converted to apk, signed with the Chamesle key and indexed. The tools are in tools/repo/.

Side project
