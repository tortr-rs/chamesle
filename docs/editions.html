Editions

Same system underneath, different desktop on top. There's one download for all of them: you pick the one you like the look of while you install. You can always add another later, for example sudo chamesle-desktop kde.

Xfce out now

The one to start with. Light on memory, and set up like Windows: one taskbar at the bottom, start menu bottom left (or press the Windows key), tray and clock bottom right. Black title bars, gray raised buttons and a gray taskbar, Oxygen icons and the Inter font.

Good for: most people, older laptops, anyone coming from Windows. 2 GB RAM is fine.

MATE out now

The desktop Linux Mint's MATE edition is built on, rock solid and very light. Same gray look as Xfce, with Mint's own start menu bottom left (the Windows key opens it too), window buttons along the bottom and the tray and clock on the right.

Good for: old machines, netbooks, people who like things to stay where they put them.

Cinnamon out now

Linux Mint's own desktop, the most polished of the Windows-style ones: smooth effects, a great start menu and pinned apps on the taskbar, just like Windows. Same gray look and the same apps underneath.

Good for: Windows switchers with a computer from the last 8 years or so. 4 GB of RAM or more.

GNOME out now

Clean, modern, and different from Windows: one top bar, an overview with all your windows, and a dock at the bottom that gets out of the way when a window needs the room. Tray icons work, windows have minimise and maximise buttons, and the touchpad clicks with a tap. Works great on touchscreens. The first edition of 0.3.

Good for: people who want something new, laptops with touchpads.

Astro out now

The first Flavor: the GNOME edition, set up for looking at the sky. Stellarium shows the sky from where you are, tonight or any night. KStars draws star charts and drives your telescope. Siril stacks and cleans up your astrophotos, and Gpredict tells you when the ISS flies over. The desktop is dark and turns warm at night, so your eyes stay used to the dark. The wallpaper is the Webb telescope's “Cosmic Cliffs” (NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI).

Good for: amateur astronomers, astrophotographers, anyone curious about what's up there. 4 GB of RAM or more.

KDE Plasma out now

A start menu, a taskbar along the bottom and the clock in the corner, so it feels familiar from the first minute. Then you can change almost anything: panels, widgets, window behaviour, the whole look. The same black title bars and gray taskbar as the others, and Dolphin for your files.

Good for: tinkerers, gamers, anyone with 4 GB of RAM or more.

Window Maker soon

The NeXTSTEP look, the real way: Window Maker, the window manager that has looked like a NeXT since the 90s. The dock sits top right with a clock, the volume and the tray in it, the menu opens wherever you right-click on the desktop, and every window has a black title bar. The apps are dressed in OneStepBack, gray with embossed buttons, by Jean-Pierre Bucciol. No taskbar and no start menu, so it takes an afternoon to get used to.

Good for: people who miss the old workstations, and anyone who wants a desktop that stays out of the way.

TTY new

A Flavor for people who like the console. Every screen is a full screen shell in the real console font and colours, and windows float on top of it in frames drawn with box characters. Super+1 to Super+9 switch between tty1 and tty9, and the bottom line shows the network, volume, brightness, battery and the time. Super+arrows put windows side by side, notifications pop up in a frame, and Super+L locks it with a login prompt. It runs on ttywm, our own window manager. The settings are one file in plain words, like super+w = firefox. Want it on another edition? Run sudo apk add ttywm and pick ttywm on the sign-in screen.

Good for: terminal people, old and slow computers, anyone who misses DOS.

Server new

Chamesle without a desktop, for a computer you reach over the network. Pick Server in Jiggity and SSH is on from the first start. Disk tools, tmux, vim and htop are in. If you ever want windows, type startx and you get ttywm.

Good for: home servers, old PCs in a cupboard, learning how Linux works underneath.

Not sure?

Get Xfce. It's the most polished one and runs on pretty much anything. MATE is just as easy, so if you already know it from Mint, pick that.
