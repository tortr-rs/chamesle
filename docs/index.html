Linux for people who just want their computer to work.

Chamesle is a desktop Linux distro. You boot it, it looks like what you're used to, and you get apps from Flathub without hunting around. If you like poking at things there's a full package manager underneath, but you never have to touch it.

Get 0.1 "Margit" Xfce edition, 64-bit, 1.8 GB

What you get

Out of the box: Firefox, a file manager, Flathub already set up, printing, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and the firmware and drivers most laptops need. An installer, an app store and a driver manager are next on the list.

Under the hood it uses dinit to start things, so it boots fast and doesn't get in your way. There's no telemetry. There's no account to sign into.

Looks familiar

Start menu in the corner, taskbar along the bottom, clock on the right. The Windows key opens the menu. You don't have to learn anything to get going.

Lots of software

Everything on Flathub, plus 14,781 packages in our own repo, from GIMP and LibreOffice to compilers and server stuff.

Runs on old stuff

The Xfce edition is happy with 2 GB of RAM. MATE is coming for even older machines.

Where it's at

0.1 is the first release. It's a live system you can boot from a USB stick and try. Next up is the installer, then switching the system over to apk and the store. The roadmap has the details.
